# Grounding — 0074 Exercise catalogue, by movement pattern

Reference only: this file states what the evidence *is*. It holds the verbatim
`## Grounding` blocks of the four science-scout runs for
[RFC 0074](../rfcs/0074-exercise-catalogue-grounded-links.md), run in parallel
on 2026-10-04 — L (lower body), P (upper push and shoulder isolation),
U (upper pull) and T (trunk, carries, Olympic lifts). Above them sits the one
table the catalogue is built from: every movement pattern with its link set.
What each fork decided is recorded in the RFC and in the
[grounding inventory](../grounding-inventory.md) ledger. Pending work never
lives here — it goes to the RFC.

Levels: 1 = prime mover, one weighted set; 2 = synergist, half a set; 3 =
stabiliser or bystander, zero (`LEVEL_WEIGHT`, inventory row 7.1). A level-3
link is kept because it documents the claim "this muscle works but does not
grow from this", and every consumer skips it.

**Overrides the runs named** (an exercise row with its own set and reason):
Single-leg RDL (Erectors 3: unilateral load); round-back back extension
(Erectors 1, Glutes 2, Hamstrings 2: the spine moves); Nordic (adds Glutes 3);
lateral lunge (Adductors 1: the trailing leg's adductors carry the lever);
sumo deadlift (adductors); bench dip (Triceps 1, Anterior Deltoid 2, Chest 3);
hammer and reverse curl, crossbody pronated curl (Biceps 1, Forearms 2:
brachioradialis is a prime flexor in that grip); face pull with external
rotation (Posterior Deltoid 1, Rotator Cuff 2, Rhomboids 2, Upper Back / Traps
2); lying pullover (Chest 1, Lats 2, Triceps 3: pec-dominant); Copenhagen plank
(Adductors 1, Obliques 2: grounded); suitcase carry (Forearms 1, Obliques 1,
Upper Back / Traps 2); snatch (Olympic + Anterior Deltoid 3); clean and jerk
(Olympic + Anterior Deltoid 2, Triceps 2).

**Granularity the runs settled:** the Isolation buckets split by joint action;
Hip Hinge splits by knee action; trunk flexion splits into spinal and hip
flexion; rotation and anti-rotation merge; the kettlebell swing joins the
straight-knee hinge (same set). Nothing splits by depth, grip, bench angle up
to 45° or knee angle in a calf raise: those move growth *inside* one of the
app's muscles, not between them.

## Grounding

### L — Lower-body pattern links

**Claim:** For each lower-body movement pattern, the set of (muscle, level) links an exercise inherits. Level 1 counts 1 set, level 2 counts 0.5, level 3 counts 0. The links feed Home's body map, the "what's missing" ranking and the 48 h recovering flag. The weights are already grounded; only which muscle gets which level is being decided here.
**Searched:** 2026-10-04 · **Verdict:** partially supported. Squat, leg press, hip thrust, knee extension, knee flexion and plantar flexion rest on per-muscle hypertrophy trials. Lunge, hip abduction, hip adduction, round-back extension, and erectors/quads in hinges are convention only.
**Number to use:** the per-pattern link sets in the table below. Default Hamstrings 3 on every knee-dominant pattern and on hip thrust, and Glutes 1 on every squat-family pattern including leg press. Those are the two places where the trials measured the muscle directly and the current rows disagree with them.

#### Evidence
- `[literature]` In full and half squats, the quads, adductors and gluteus maximus all grew. Full squats: knee extensors +4.9%, adductors +6.2%, glute max +6.7%. Half squats: +4.6%, +2.7%, +2.2%. **Hamstrings did not change in either group**, and neither did rectus femoris. RCT, MRI volume, 10 wk, untrained men, n=17 (Kubo, Ikebukuro & Yata 2019). [Kubo 2019 PDF](https://highfit.com.br/wp-content/uploads/2019/06/KEITARO-KUBO-2019.pdf)
- `[literature]` Hip thrust and back squat grew the gluteus maximus by about the same amount (differences negligible in every region). Squat grew the quads (+3.6 cm²) and adductors (+2.5 cm²) more than hip thrust did. **Hamstrings grew negligibly in both** (0.1 ± 0.6 cm² difference). Glute med/min grew little or not at all. RCT, MRI CSA, 9 wk, untrained adults, n=34 (HT 18, SQ 16) (Plotkin et al. 2023). [Front Physiol](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physiology/articles/10.3389/fphys.2023.1279170/full)
- `[literature]` Leg press vs knee extension. Leg press: **glute max +15.4%**, adductor magnus +6.2%, vasti +4.4–6.2%, rectus femoris +1.1% (ns). Knee extension: rectus femoris +13.2%, vasti comparable, no glute or adductor growth (+0.2%, −0.9%). Biceps femoris long head grew slightly in both conditions (+5.3% / +2.0%, no difference between them), which the authors call far below what leg curls produce. Within-subject (one leg per exercise), MRI, 12 wk, untrained adults, n=17 (Kinoshita et al. 2026, MSSE). [MSSE ahead of print](https://www.fisiologiadelejercicio.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/hypertrophic_effects_of_single__versus_multi_joint.1002-1.pdf) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41630124/)
- `[literature]` Resistance training has a moderate effect on glute max hypertrophy (SMD 0.71). Full-range squats and hip thrusts reliably grow it. Leg press results are mixed and depend on machine, range of motion and foot position. Stiff-leg deadlifts appear only inside combined protocols, and lunges were not isolated. Systematic review and meta-analysis, 12 studies (11 pooled), n=318 (Krause Neto et al. 2025). [Front Physiol](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physiology/articles/10.3389/fphys.2025.1542334/full)
- `[literature]` Stiff-leg deadlift grew the whole hamstrings +7.0% (semimembranosus +11.2%) against +1.2% in controls. Nordic grew them +11.4% (semitendinosus +24.3%). Blinded RCT, MRI, 9 wk, untrained students, n=36 (12 per arm) (Morin et al., medRxiv 2024, later indexed on PubMed). [medRxiv](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.12.17.24319207v1.full) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40644669/)
- `[literature]` The 45° hip extension (a flat-back back extension) and the Nordic both increased semitendinosus volume. Hip extension grew biceps femoris long head volume more than the Nordic (p=0.037). RCT, MRI, 10 wk, recreationally active men, n=30 (Bourne et al. 2016/17). [Univ. South Wales record](https://pure.southwales.ac.uk/en/publications/impact-of-the-nordic-hamstring-and-hip-extension-exercises-on-ham/)
- `[literature]` Seated leg curl grew the whole hamstrings +14.1% and lying (prone) leg curl +9.3%. Both grew them substantially. **The gastrocnemius was not measured.** Within-subject RCT, MRI, 12 wk, untrained adults, n=20 (Maeo et al. 2021). [Full text](https://uniguacu.com.br/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Artigo-Hipertrofia-muscular-dos-isquiotibiais-maiores.pdf) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33009197/)
- `[literature]` Standing calf raise grew the gastrocnemius +9–12% and seated grew it +0.6–1.7%. The soleus grew about the same in both (+2.1% vs +2.9%). Within-subject, MRI, 12 wk, untrained adults, n=14 (Kinoshita et al. 2023). [Front Physiol](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physiology/articles/10.3389/fphys.2023.1272106/full)
- `[literature]` I found no training study measuring per-muscle hypertrophy after lunges or split squats only, after hip abduction or adduction machines only, after round-back extensions, or measuring erector growth after deadlifts. The search covered the reviews above plus targeted queries. Those links rest on joint mechanics and EMG, not growth data.
- `[single-practitioner position]` Squat, hack and leg-press variations with different stances train the adductors as well as the quads. RP Strength (Israetel's company, unsigned article) only; no other roster member located on this. [RP quad training](https://rpstrength.com/blogs/articles/quad-hypertrophy-training-tips)
- I located no roster position specific enough to tag on hamstrings-in-squats, calves-in-leg-curls, erectors-in-deadlifts or glute crediting for abduction. The practitioner layer is silent here; it does not agree.

#### Where they split
- **Adductors in the squat family: level 2 or level 1?** By growth, the adductors keep pace with the prime movers. Adductor magnus grew +6.2% in full squats (Kubo) and in leg press (Kinoshita), as much as or more than the quads. Adductor longus and brevis did not grow (Kinoshita). The app has one Adductors leaf, so its level has to stand for the whole group. **Default 2** (as 0042 decided). Moving to 1 would credit longus and brevis with growth the trials did not find. The choice for Tekiō: does the Adductors leaf track the magnus (then 1) or the group (then 2)?
- **Erectors in hinges.** Isometric brace means level 3 is the 0042 rule, applied to squats and single-leg RDL. Bilateral hinges put the largest lumbar extensor moment of any lift on the erectors, and the live Deadlift row already says Erectors 1. No trial measures erector growth either way. **Default 2 for bilateral hinges, 3 for single-leg RDL and squats.** This is convention, and the reason is a moment-arm argument. Tekiō has to choose whether "isometric" alone sends a muscle to 3, or whether load magnitude can lift it to 2.
- **Glutes in leg press.** Kinoshita 2026 found +15.4%, but the meta-analysis calls leg press glute results mixed and setup-dependent. Glutes 1 follows the one direct MRI trial. If foot position were ever captured, it would be the variable that matters, and the catalogue does not capture it.
- **Hip abduction → Glutes 1.** The app's Glutes leaf is, by volume, mostly gluteus maximus. Abduction mainly loads the medius and minimus, and those barely grew in Plotkin and not at all in Kinoshita's multi-joint arms. Crediting a full Glutes set for abduction is a convention that lets the one leaf represent three muscles. The alternative, Glutes 2, has no data behind it either.
- **Granularity.** Split Hip Hinge into *straight-knee* (RDL, stiff-leg DL, good morning) and *knee-involved* (conventional/trap-bar deadlift: adds Quads 2). That changes the link set. Split hip-extension isolation, hip abduction and hip adduction into three patterns. Do **not** split squat by depth, leg curl by seated vs lying, or calf raise by knee angle. Those change how much a muscle grows, not which muscles are linked, and the app has no sub-muscle leaves. Sumo deadlift (adductors) and lateral lunge (Adductors 1) are exercise-level overrides, each with a one-line reason.

#### Caveats
- Population mismatch: every hypertrophy trial here used untrained young adults (n 14–36) over 9–12 weeks. In trained lifters, synergist growth (adductors in squat, glutes in leg press) is likely smaller in absolute terms, and the null results (hamstrings in squat and hip thrust) are unlikely to reverse. A trained adult who squats deep with a wide stance may shift the adductors; one who leg-presses with a high foot may shift the glutes. The catalogue cannot see either.
- Calves 3 in the leg curl drops a live level-2 link. The gastrocnemius is a knee flexor, so the mechanism is real, but no growth trial measured it. A 2026 ultrasound swelling study of seated leg curl by ankle position exists ([JSCR, PubMed 41609763](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41609763/)). Only its title was readable, so it is not cited for any number.
- What would move this number: a per-muscle MRI trial of lunges or split squats; any trial measuring erector growth after deadlift or RDL; a hip-abduction training trial with MRI of the glutes; or a leg-curl trial that also measures the gastrocnemius. Any of these could move lunge Hamstrings, hinge Erectors, abduction Glutes or leg-curl Calves by one level.

### P — Upper-body push and shoulder-isolation pattern links

**Claim:** For each upper-body push and shoulder-isolation pattern, the set of (muscle, level) links that every exercise in that pattern inherits. Level 1 is 1 weighted set, level 2 is 0.5, level 3 is 0 (the weights were grounded in 039 S1 and 0042 and are not re-run here). The link set feeds three things: Home's body map, the "what's missing" ranking and the 48 h recovering flag.
**Searched:** 2026-10-04 · **Verdict:** partially supported. Horizontal push and elbow extension are **supported**. Vertical push, shoulder abduction and chest fly are **partially supported**: the prime movers are measured, but most synergist links rest on EMG or anatomy. Front raise, shrug, dips and bench dip are **convention only**.
**Number to use:** see the table. Every link is a 1 / 2 / 3 choice, so there is no band to give. Where the evidence does not pin a link, the default is the level that does not hide a gap. A "what's missing" read must not credit stimulus nobody has measured (039 S1).

#### Evidence
- `[literature]` Bench press alone for 10 weeks grew the pectoralis major, pectoralis minor, **anterior deltoid** and **triceps** more than control. The pec grew more than the triceps. The **medial deltoid** was the one measured muscle not reported to grow. This is the anchor for horizontal push: Chest 1, Anterior Deltoid 2, Triceps 2, Lateral Deltoid 3. RCT, 24 men (13 bench only, 11 control), 10 weeks. Abstract-level claim carried from 039 S1, where the PMID was checked via eutils; I could not reopen the abstract this run — [Lanza et al. 2024, J Bodyw Mov Ther](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39593465/)
- `[literature]` Bench press vs skull crusher: bench grew the lateral head of the triceps more, extensions grew the long head more, and the medial head was similar. Real but partial triceps stimulus from pressing supports level 2. RCT, 43 untrained young men, 10 weeks — [Brandão et al. 2020, J Strength Cond Res](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32149887/) (carried from 039 S1)
- `[literature]` Push-up and bench press at matched load (~40 % 1RM) both increased pectoralis major and triceps thickness, with no group difference. Push-ups belong in the horizontal-push pattern. RCT, 18 recreationally trained collegiate men (9 / 9), 8 weeks — [Kikuchi & Nakazato 2017, J Exerc Sci Fit](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1728869X17301028)
- `[literature]` Incline vs flat bench: the only muscle-thickness site that differed was the upper pec (2nd intercostal space), favouring incline (+0.62 cm vs horizontal). **Only the pectoralis major was measured.** No anterior deltoid or triceps data, so no trial supports promoting the anterior deltoid to level 1 for incline. (The caller's "Chaves 2024" appears to be this 2020 paper; I found no 2024 Chaves incline trial.) RCT, 47 untrained men, 3 groups, 8 weeks, 1×/week — [Chaves et al. 2020, Int J Exerc Sci](https://digitalcommons.wku.edu/ijes/vol13/iss6/12) (PubMed [32922646](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32922646/))
- `[literature]` Across five bench angles, upper-pec EMG peaked at 30°. Anterior-deltoid EMG peaked at 60° and was significantly higher at any incline above 45°. This is EMG, not growth. It puts a soft line at ~45°: at or below it the lift is horizontal push, and steeper presses behave more like vertical push. Repeated measures, 30 trained adults — [Rodríguez-Ridao et al. 2020, IJERPH](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7579505/)
- `[literature]` Bench grip width: pec activation was similar across narrow, medium and wide grips. Triceps activation was lower only with the *wide* grip, with narrow and medium similar. The anterior deltoid was higher with medium grip than narrow. Close-grip bench shifts emphasis but not the link set. Within-subject crossover, 28 men (15 resistance-trained, 13 novice), 6RM, EMG — [Saeterbakken et al. 2021, IJERPH](https://www.mdpi.com/1660-4601/18/12/6444) (PubMed [34198674](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34198674/))
- `[literature]` Overhead triceps extension vs cable pushdown, same sets and reps: overhead grew the long head more (+28.5 % vs +19.6 %) and the lateral and medial heads more (+14.6 % vs +10.5 %). Both grew the triceps and nothing else is claimed. This is emphasis within one leaf muscle, so no link change. Within-subject unilateral, 21 untrained adults, 12 weeks, MRI. Figures as reported by [Stronger by Science](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/research-spotlight-triceps/); the primary is [Maeo et al. 2023, Eur J Sport Sci](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1080/17461391.2022.2100279), which returned 403 to me
- `[literature]` Dumbbell vs cable lateral raises grew the lateral deltoid similarly (+3.3–4.6 % at proximal and distal sites). Only the lateral deltoid was measured, so this grounds Lateral Deltoid 1 and says nothing about Anterior Deltoid 2. Within-participant RCT, 24 resistance-trained men and women, 8 weeks, ultrasound — [Larsen et al. 2025, Front Physiol](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physiology/articles/10.3389/fphys.2025.1611468/full)
- `[literature]` In lateral-raise variants vs front raise, the front raise activated the anterior deltoid and pectoralis major most. The neutral-grip lateral raise maximised the medial deltoid, and the externally rotated lateral raise raised anterior and medial deltoid activation. Anterior deltoid in lateral raises and chest in front raises are therefore EMG-only claims. Crossover EMG, 10 competitive bodybuilders — [Coratella et al. 2020, IJERPH](https://zenodo.org/records/5337073) (PubMed [32824894](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32824894/))
- `[literature]` Front vs back and barbell vs machine overhead press: all four variants excite the anterior, medial and posterior deltoid, upper trapezius, pec and triceps. Back presses excite the medial deltoid more, and upper-trapezius excitation did not differ between variants. This supports Lateral Deltoid 2 and Traps 2 as present in every OHP variant, at EMG level only. Crossover EMG, 8 male competitive bodybuilders — [Coratella et al. 2022, Front Physiol](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physiology/articles/10.3389/fphys.2022.825880/full) (PubMed [35936912](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35936912/))
- `[literature]` Upright row: lateral- and posterior-deltoid activity rise with grip width, the anterior deltoid is unchanged across grips, upper and middle trapezius are active with grip-dependent eccentric differences, and biceps activity falls with wider grips. This supports Lateral Deltoid 1, Traps 2 and Anterior Deltoid 2, at EMG level. Within-subject EMG, 16 weight-trained men — [McAllister et al. 2013, J Strength Cond Res](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22362088/) ([full text](https://highfit.com.br/wp-content/uploads/2017/02/MCALLISTER-2013.pdf))
- `[literature]` Pec deck alone grew clavicular (+17–18 %) and sternocostal (+21 %) pec thickness, the same with 1 or 3 sets. Only the pec was measured, so this grounds Chest 1 for horizontal adduction. Anterior Deltoid 2 rests on the shoulder action it shares with bench, where Lanza 2024 measured anterior-deltoid growth. Within-subject, 15 untrained young men, 12 weeks, ultrasound — [Pinto et al. 2025, J Strength Cond Res](https://ro.ecu.edu.au/ecuworks2022-2026/6292/) (PubMed [40266636](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40266636/))
- `[literature]` I located no training trial that measures per-muscle growth for dips, bench dips, front raises, shrugs or the overhead press. Those link sets have no hypertrophy data.
- `[practitioner consensus]` Pressing gives the anterior deltoid real growth stimulus: "the front delts get most or even all of the stimulus they require from chest exercises", with front-delt MV and MEV at 0–2 sets, and removing pressing raises the triceps landmarks substantially. Held by Israetel ([RP front-delt guide](https://rpstrength.com/blogs/articles/front-delt-hypertrophy-training-tips), [RP triceps guide](https://rpstrength.com/blogs/articles/triceps-hypertrophy-training-tips)). Galpin's general indirect-work position is recorded in 039 S1. Lanza 2024 and Brandão 2020 are consistent with it.
- `[single-practitioner position]` Side delts need direct work. RP puts side-delt MEV at 6–8 sets while front-delt MEV is 0–2, and uses upright rows as a primary side-delt lift — implying pressing credits the side delt far less than the front delt ([RP side-delt guide](https://rpstrength.com/blogs/articles/side-delt-hypertrophy-training-tips)). Israetel only. Galpin was silent in what I searched.
- `[single-practitioner position]` Cavaliere and Conor Harris were not consulted for any link. They are coaches and the rule bars them from grounding a classification.

#### Where they split
**1. Lateral deltoid in the overhead press: 2 or 3.** EMG (Coratella 2022) shows the medial deltoid working in every OHP variant, more so behind the neck. Israetel's landmarks treat side delts as needing direct work, which implies pressing contributes little. The one growth measurement is Lanza's bench press, where the medial deltoid did not grow, but bench has almost no abduction, so it does not transfer cleanly. Tekiō has to choose: credit OHP half a side-delt set (default **2**, live value, EMG-backed), or drop it to 3 and let lateral raises alone fill the side delt. With 3, Home may call the side delt missing for someone who presses overhead a lot. With 2, it may call it covered when Israetel's model says it isn't. Neither side has a growth trial, so the default stays where the EMG points and the link is marked partially supported.

**2. Anterior deltoid in lateral raises (and chest in front raises): EMG-only synergists.** 0042 kept Lateral Raise → Anterior Deltoid at 2 as borderline. The evidence has not moved: Coratella 2020 is EMG, and no growth trial measured it. I propose the front raise's Chest link at **3** rather than 2, because crediting the whole chest half a set for a clavicular-pec EMG signal hides a chest gap. That is less generous than the lateral-raise → anterior-delt link, which has the same kind of evidence. Tekiō should either accept the inconsistency, which is gentler on the delts where pressing already covers them, or drop both to 3. Do not move either to 1.

**3. Dips: Triceps 1 (live) or Triceps 2 (pattern).** No study separates dips from bench for per-muscle growth. Pelland's classification makes the triceps an indirect mover in any compound press. Lifters and the EMG lore (Bagchi, located but not read) treat dips as triceps-heavy. My default is **2**, folding dips into horizontal push. If triceps 1 is kept, it has to be written as a convention-backed variant override with its reason. Do not split the difference.

**4. Incline: no split by default.** No trial measures anterior-deltoid growth from incline pressing. Chaves 2020 measured only the pec, and the anterior-delt rise above 45° is EMG. Standard incline (~30–45°) inherits horizontal push. Steep incline (> 45°) may be filed under vertical push. That changes the set (Anterior Deltoid 1, Chest drops out or goes to 2), and it is a convention for the catalogue to state, not a finding.

#### Caveats
- Population mismatch: almost every growth trial here is in young men, mostly untrained, n = 15–47, over 8–12 weeks. The exceptions are Larsen 2025 (trained men and women) and Kikuchi 2017 (recreationally trained). EMG data come from small groups of bodybuilders (n = 8–10). For one trained adult the prime-mover links are solid. The synergist links (OHP lateral delt, lateral-raise anterior delt, fly anterior delt, upright-row traps) are inferences from EMG or from shared joint action, not measurements.
- Leaf-group granularity hides the real effect of most variants: incline (upper pec), close-grip (triceps emphasis) and overhead vs pushdown (long head, +45 % more growth) all shift growth *inside* a leaf muscle. None of them changes the (muscle, level) set, which is why none of them earns a split.
- Fetch limits: the Europe PMC search API (one URL bundling PMIDs 32922646, 39593465, 40692697 and 36334406) was refused with HTTP 429, and I did not retry it. Lanza 2024's numbers are carried from the eutils-verified 039 S1 entry, not re-read today. The Maeo 2023 primary returned 403, so its figures come from a secondary write-up. Ekstrom 2003 (JOSPT; shrug and upper-trap EMG) was located but could not be opened, so it is not used.
- What would move this: a training trial measuring all three deltoid heads after OHP-only work (would settle split 1); one measuring the anterior deltoid after lateral raises only, or the anterior deltoid and triceps after dips (splits 2 and 3); an incline-only trial with anterior-deltoid ultrasound (split 4); filling `exercise_muscle_groups.contribution` per link (the upgrade path named in 039 S1).

### U — Upper-body pull pattern links

**Claim:** For each upper-body pull pattern, the (muscle, link level) set every exercise in that pattern inherits. These links drive Home's muscle body map, the "what's missing" ranking and the 48 h recovering flag. The rule in question is "elbow flexion does not train the lats", along with the other five pull patterns.

#### Evidence
- `[literature]` Biceps in a row is a real synergist at about half a set: unilateral dumbbell rows grew elbow-flexor thickness +5.16 % against +11.06 % for curls, a ratio of ≈0.47. Within-subject, 10 untrained men, 8 weeks — [Mannarino et al. 2021, J Strength Cond Res](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31268995/) (verified in 039 S1)
- `[literature]` Rows grew the elbow flexors proximally (5 %, matching curls) but barely distally (1 % against 11 %). Chronic arm, 9 untrained men, 8 weeks — [Leitão et al. 2025, Sport Sci Health](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11332-025-01530-7) (from 039 S1)
- `[literature]` Biceps in a pulldown programme: lat pulldown plus bench grew elbow-flexor thickness +6.10 %, against +5.83 % for curls and extensions. That is ≈1.0 of a direct set, which supports at least level 2. It does not support level 1: Pelland's "direct" requires the primary force generator. RCT, 29 untrained men, 10 weeks — [Gentil, Soares & Bottaro 2015, Asian J Sports Med](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26446291/) (verified in 039 S1)
- `[literature]` The lats themselves grow from a pull-heavy programme: latissimus dorsi biopsy fibre CSA rose about 22 % overall. The programme was bench press, lat pulldown, seated row, shoulder press, hammer curl and external rotation, so pulldown and row cannot be separated from each other or from the rest. This is the only lat-growth trial located. Randomised (protein vs control), 18 novice men, 8 weeks — [Paoli et al. 2016, Nutrients](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27258300/) ([full text](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6643/8/6/331))
- `[literature]` The seated row recruits middle trapezius and rhomboids more than either pulldown, and actively retracting the scapula did not raise it further. The protracted-scapula row also beat both pulldowns for lat activation. Wide-pronated and supinated pulldowns differed "very little". Biceps activation did not differ across pulldowns and rows. EMG crossover, 12 trained men — [Lehman et al. 2004, Dynamic Medicine](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/1476-5918-3-4)
- `[literature]` In the low-pulley row the trapezius "seems to be the muscle that is most activated" (transverse trapezius up to ≈81 % MVC), posterior deltoid reached ≈77 % MVC, and lat activation did not differ across 9 grip and width combinations. EMG crossover, 12 trained men — [Taboada-Iglesias et al. 2024, Retos 61](https://www.revistaretos.org/index.php/retos/article/download/107771/79975/440643)
- `[literature]` A synthesis of 23 EMG studies. The pulldown's front bar path most consistently raises lat EMG, and grip width effects are inconsistent. A narrow-grip row favours the lats, a wide grip raises the upper trapezius. In pull-ups a supinated grip raises biceps and a pronated grip raises lower trapezius, with lats comparable across grips. The pullover produced "greater pectoralis major than latissimus dorsi EMG amplitude". None of the included studies measured hypertrophy. Narrative review, 23 studies — [di Cagno et al. 2026, J Funct Morphol Kinesiol](https://www.mdpi.com/2411-5142/11/3/315) ([PubMed 42647355](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42647355/))
- `[literature]` Latissimus dorsi and triceps "showed the highest EMG activity in the straight arm pulldown". The barbell pullover beat the straight-arm pulldown in every muscle measured except those two, chest most of all. EMG crossover, 20 resistance-trained adults — [Muyor, López-Miñarro & Alacid 2022, Appl Sci](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/12/21/11138)
- `[literature]` Horizontal abduction with external rotation is a posterior-deltoid exercise: prone horizontal abduction at 100° with full ER gave the highest posterior deltoid activity (88 % MVIC) in the set. Infraspinatus and teres minor peaked in a different exercise, sidelying ER (62 % and 67 % MVIC). EMG, 10 healthy adults — [Reinold et al. 2004, JOSPT](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15296366/)
- `[literature]` Brachioradialis is active in every curl grip. In this sample both biceps and brachioradialis excitation were highest with a supinated grip (biceps +19 % over pronated; brachioradialis +5 %). EMG crossover, 10 competitive male bodybuilders — [Coratella et al. 2023, Sports](https://www.mdpi.com/2075-4663/11/3/64). No training study measuring brachioradialis or forearm growth from curls was located.
- `[literature]` The latissimus dorsi inserts on the intertubercular groove of the humerus and acts at the shoulder (extension, adduction, internal rotation). It does not cross the elbow, so elbow flexion cannot load it. This is an anatomical reference, not a study — [Physiopedia, Latissimus Dorsi](https://www.physio-pedia.com/Latissimus_Dorsi_Muscle)

This is the definition every level above is judged against. Bayesian meta-regression, 67 studies, n = 2,058 — [Pelland et al. 2026, Sports Med](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41343037/) (verified in 039 S1)

Israetel/RP only; Galpin is silent on rear delts. — [RP rear-delt guide](https://rpstrength.com/blogs/articles/rear-delt-hypertrophy-training-tips), [RP 11-set rule page](https://rpstrength.com/blogs/podcasts/training-frequency-decoded-the-11-set-rule-every-lifter-should-know)
- `[single-practitioner position]` Traps "get tons of stimulus from side delt, front delt, rear delt, and back work", enough that most people grow them "with zero direct trap training". Forearms "maintain and minimally grow" from back, shoulder and biceps work, and wrist curls are the direct forearm exercise. Israetel/RP only — [RP trap guide](https://rpstrength.com/blogs/articles/trap-hypertrophy-training-tips), [RP forearm guide](https://rpstrength.com/blogs/articles/forearm-hypertrophy-training-tips)

That is a practitioner reason to keep two patterns. Israetel/RP only — [RP back guide](https://rpstrength.com/blogs/articles/back-hypertrophy-training-tips)

This is the level-2 biceps call — [Galpin, Huberman Lab guest series pt 2](https://podcastnotes.org/huberman-lab/guest-series-dr-andy-galpin-optimal-protocols-to-build-strength-grow-muscles-part-2-huberman-lab/) (from 039 S1)

The only EMG located puts the posterior deltoid at the top for horizontal abduction with ER (Reinold 2004). That makes "Rotator Cuff 1" a convention.

#### Where they split
1. **Mid-back in rows: level 1 or level 2?** The EMG says the trapezius is the most activated muscle in a row (Taboada-Iglesias) and that rows recruit mid-trap and rhomboids more than pulldowns (Lehman). Under Pelland's definition that makes them co-primary force generators, so level 1. Israetel's bookkeeping puts trap stimulus from back work in the indirect column, and RP's row example credits lats 1.0 and leaves the mid-back unnamed. No hypertrophy trial measures trapezius or rhomboid growth from rows. **Tekiō must choose:** level 1, where mid-back reads fed on rows alone, or level 2, where a rows-only week leaves mid-back at half and the map asks for direct work nobody can show is needed. The EMG leans to 1, and that is the default. If the caller prefers the conservative read, level 2 is defensible. Do not split the difference with a per-link 0.75: nobody holds it.
2. **Face pull: rotator cuff or rear delt?** The coaching framing (Cavaliere) is rotator cuff. The EMG (Reinold) and RP's bookkeeping are posterior deltoid. **Tekiō must choose** whether the face pull keeps its own row or joins the reverse-fly set. The evidence supports the override with Posterior Deltoid 1 and Rotator Cuff 2. Keeping Rotator Cuff 1 is a convention, and it is the only thing currently making the cuff read "fed".
3. **Straight-arm pulldown vs pullover.** Same joint action, different prime mover in the EMG: lats in the cable version, chest in the lying pullover (Muyor, di Cagno). One shoulder-extension pattern with the pullover as an override is cheaper than two patterns. The ambiguous row has to be resolved by name first. "PJR Pullover" is described across trade and coaching pages ([example](https://www.boxrox.com/is-the-pjr-pullover-the-perfect-triceps-exercise-for-super-strong-and-muscular-arms/)) as a lying elbow-extension variant, so a convention and not evidence. If that is what was logged, it belongs in **elbow extension** (Triceps 1, Lats 3), and "Cable Extension" needs a session check to tell a cable triceps extension from a straight-arm pulldown. Do not keep both meanings on one row: it is the *Lat Raises* error again.

#### Caveats
- Population mismatch: the only hypertrophy data here are biceps-from-pulling trials in untrained men (n = 9–29, 8–10 weeks) and one novice lat-biopsy trial (n = 18). For one trained adult, trained men in Mannarino-type trials still show the biceps synergy (de França 2015, in 039 S1). The back tiers are informed classification, not measurement.
- Level 3 counts 0 sets, so Posterior Deltoid 3 on vertical pulls and Forearms 3 on curls only matter if the 48 h recovering flag reads level-3 links. Check how `RECOVER_DAYS` treats level 3 before shipping, or a pulldown will flag rear delts as recovering with zero credited stimulus.
- Rotator Cuff and Forearms have no compound-lift growth data at all. Whether they belong on a 10-set-floor map is a design question this run cannot answer.
- Not read: Europe PMC rate-limited this run (HTTP 429), and the coordinator then asked for no new pages. So the abstracts of Kleiber et al. 2015 (PubMed 26300781, brachioradialis by hand position), Marchetti & Uchida 2011 (21975179, pullover EMG) and PubMed 24947920 (deltoid heads in single- vs multi-joint lifts) are not cited. The pullover's pec dominance rests on Muyor 2022 and di Cagno 2026 instead.

Posterior-deltoid growth from rows alone would settle the 2-vs-3 call on rows. Brachioradialis growth by curl grip would settle the hammer-curl override. ### T — Trunk, carry and Olympic pattern links

**Claim:** For each trunk, carry and Olympic/ballistic pattern, the set of (muscle, level) links an exercise inherits. Level 1 counts 1 set per set, level 2 counts 0.5, level 3 counts 0. **Granularity:** split trunk flexion into *spinal flexion* and *hip-flexion trunk*. The split changes Hip Flexors 3 ↔ 1, which is the biggest single change in this set. Merge rotation with anti-rotation, because the link set is the same. Keep anti-extension and lateral flexion separate, because the level-1 muscle differs. Copenhagen is a variant override inside lateral flexion, with a reason. Unilateral and bilateral carries differ (Obliques 1 vs 3), so keep them as two rows or one pattern with an override. The swing has no link-set difference from the hinge today, so it merges. Snatch and clean-and-jerk are overrides of one Olympic pattern, not separate patterns.

#### Evidence
- `[literature]` 18 weeks of trunk-strength training increased MRI cross-sectional area of the psoas (P<0.001) and of the back muscles, erector spinae and multifidus (P<0.01). Size gains did not track strength gains. Training to flex the trunk grows the hip flexors, which supports Hip Flexors 1 on sit-ups and leg raises. Design: uncontrolled pre/post, sedentary students (11 F, 1 M), n=12. Parkkola, Kujala & Rytökoski 1992 — [Eur J Appl Physiol](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF00243501)
- `[literature]` During a crunch the rectus abdominis thickened 36% more than in a leg raise, and during a sit-up 32% more. It shortened and thickened in every exercise tested, and recruitment was "biased closer to the load". So the RA works in leg raises but less than in a crunch, and both patterns keep RA 1. Design: acute, randomised order, EMG + ultrasound, n=15. Not a training study. Gomirato & Grenier 2023 — [IJES 16(1)](https://digitalcommons.wku.edu/ijes/vol16/iss1/10)
- `[literature]` 4 weeks of prone plank (5×/wk) increased RA, external oblique and internal oblique thickness more than reverse plank or bridge did. Design: three-group pre/post, no untrained control, healthy young adults, n=30. Weak: short, small, and the reported values read oddly (EO "78.9 mm"). This is the only isometric anti-extension training trial with a thickness outcome that I found. Ko & Song 2018 — [PTRS 7(2)](https://www.jptrs.org/journal/view.html?doi=10.14474/ptrs.2018.7.2.72)
- `[literature]` 8 weeks of Copenhagen adduction training increased adductor longus thickness by about 18% in each leg (d≈1.8, p<0.001), with no change in controls. The gain reversed after 4 weeks of detraining. This is a held, side-plank-style position growing the muscle that resists the moment. It is the strongest support for the bracing rule. Design: RCT, active recreational men, n=45. Alonso-Fernández et al. 2022 — [IJERPH 19(11):6563](https://www.mdpi.com/1660-4601/19/11/6563)
- `[literature]` Over 8 weeks, Olympic-style weightlifting produced only trivial vastus lateralis and rectus femoris thickness change (~3%). Motorised strength/power training gave 6.1% and free-weight strength/power training gave 3.8–5.4% (small effects). Olympic lifting was "generally ineffective" for jump and sprint too. Design: RCT, young athletes, n=39 (13 per group). Helland et al. 2017, MSSE — [abstract](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/309762750_Training_Strategies_to_Improve_Muscle_Power_Is_Olympic-style_Weightlifting_Relevant)
- `[literature]` In a farmer's walk, peak EMG was highest in the lats (152–169% MVC), then lumbar erectors (106–144%), internal oblique (81–111%) and glute max (114%), with RA low (13–21%). In a suitcase carry the obliques fire asymmetrically, so the oblique opposite the load does the work. Design: acute EMG, peak values, n=3 (one strongman). Direction only, not growth. McGill, McDermott & Fenwick 2009 — [JSCR, PubMed 19528856](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19528856/)
- `[literature]` Precedent, already grounded: in squat trials, minor contributors that were measured (hamstrings in squats) did not grow. Isometric bracers were set to level 3 = 0. — [0042](https://github.com/petrovsco/tekio.rfcs/blob/develop/rfcs/done/0042-level-3-link-audit.md), via 039 S1
- `[single-practitioner position]` Abs get meaningful indirect work from heavy squats and deadlifts, so direct ab volume can start low. This is Mike Israetel / RP's ab volume-landmark framing. I recalled it and did not re-read it this session; the RP page is at [rpstrength.com/expert-advice/ab-training](https://rpstrength.com/expert-advice/ab-training). No training study I found measures RA growth from compounds alone. Galpin, Attia and the others are silent on link levels.

Searched and **not found**: any hypertrophy trial of loaded carries (grip, traps or obliques), kettlebell swings, Pallof press or woodchops, or dynamic crunch training with a thickness outcome that I could read. Some pages were located but **not read**: the crunch-protocol trial (PubMed 37621710), the rotational-exercise RCT (PubMed 34391272), Lake & Lauder 2012 (kettlebell swing, strength outcomes), Thorborg 2016 (hip-flexor RCT, strength outcomes) and Yamanaka 2021 (psoas training in runners). Proxy/robots/429 blocks stopped them, and a Europe PMC search was rate-limited. None of them is cited for a number.

#### Where they split
- **Is bracing a stimulus?** The evidence splits here; the roster is silent. 0042's precedent (squat → Erectors 0) treats bracing as no stimulus. Copenhagen (Alonso-Fernández 2022) and plank (Ko & Song 2018) show held positions do grow the muscle resisting the moment. Both are true. The fork Tekiō has to settle is *which muscle resists the external moment*. The bracing rule above settles it, and it is a modelling choice that the scout cannot measure. If Tekiō instead credits every bracer at 2, the Erectors and Obliques read well-fed from squats and deadlifts alone. That is the rejected 0042 position, and it would contradict a shipped decision.
- **Carries: credit or not.** The one data source (McGill 2009, n=3) points at the lats, erectors and glutes. The convention ("carries build grip and traps", which is lifter lore) points at forearms and traps. Neither has a growth trial behind it. Option A: everything at 3, so carries are conditioning and count nothing on the map. That is honest, but a logged carry would read as nothing done. Option B (default): Forearms 1 and Traps 2. This is convention, chosen because grip is what limits the task, and it contradicts the forearm precedent from pull-ups and curls, where grip is incidental rather than limiting. Peter's call. Do not average to "Forearms 2".
- **Olympic lifts as hypertrophy.** Helland 2017 says the thigh barely grows from Olympic lifting compared with traditional training. That challenges Quadriceps 1 and Glutes 1. But the mismatch is mostly in *set counting*, because Olympic sets are low-rep and far from failure, more than in *which muscles move*. Tekiō has two options: keep Glutes and Quadriceps at 1 and accept that Olympic sets over-read on the map, or drop both to 2. Default: keep them at 1 and leave the effort question to the rep-band and quality classification, which is not part of this decision.

#### Caveats
- Population mismatch: every trunk study here is on untrained or sedentary adults or rehab populations, mostly over 4–8 weeks. Gains that early in a trunk muscle in an untrained person will overstate what a trained lifter gets. The Olympic trial used athletes but the training lasted only 8 weeks. The carry EMG has n=3. None of it separates the sexes, and none of it addresses trunk volume that is already high from compound lifts.
- Vocabulary limits: quadratus lumborum, transversus abdominis and glute medius are not leaf groups. So lateral flexion and carries cannot credit the muscle that most likely does the work. Obliques are the nearest leaf.
- What would move this number: an ultrasound or MRI training trial of loaded carries (Forearms and Traps), Pallof press or woodchop (Obliques), or squat/deadlift-only training measuring RA and obliques (that would test the bracing rule's level 3). The Erectors decision in the Hip Hinge scout run sets the Erectors link for the Olympic pattern and the kettlebell swing.
